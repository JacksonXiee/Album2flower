# Florist-buildable visual translation

Preserve the source image's visual logic while designing a bouquet that a florist could actually source, bind, wrap, and hold.

## 1. Assign palette roles

Name only the roles present in the image:

| Image role | Typical buildable implementation |
|---|---|
| Dominant field | Largest wrapping/backing-paper plane or repeated filler mass |
| Secondary mass | Main flower family or second paper layer |
| Highlight | One controlled focal flower cluster, translucent layer, or negative space |
| Accent | A few buds, flower centers, dyed tips, ribbon, or narrow paper edge |
| Dark anchor | Real branches, deep foliage, naturally dark blooms, ribbon, or outer paper |

As a starting relationship—not a quota—use roughly 45–60% dominant, 20–35% secondary, 5–15% accent/highlight, and only enough dark anchor to reproduce contrast. Put large unnatural color fields into paper rather than forcing every flower into an artificial color.

## 2. Map composition into bouquet mechanics

- Branches, handwriting-like strokes, cracks, or diagonals → flowering branches, contorted willow, dried vines, grasses, or asymmetrical line work.
- One large subject → one focal bloom family with restrained support flowers.
- Repeated dots or halftone → berries, button flowers, statice, baby's breath, or perforated paper; preserve rhythm without printing the source artwork.
- Water, haze, glass, or reflections → florist-safe translucent cellophane, glassine, organza, airy flowers, or moving shadows.
- Large empty field → sparse spacing and visible paper planes; do not fill every gap with foliage.
- Dense texture → layered bloom sizes and paper, while retaining one readable focal hierarchy.
- Strong border or black frame → dark outer sheet, branch skeleton, or narrow ribbon edge.
- Upward movement → tall narrow silhouette. Radial movement → open round form. Diagonal movement → asymmetric fan or crescent.
- Hard geometry → fewer large flower shapes and crisp paper folds. Soft painterly imagery → tonal clusters, tissue, vellum-like film, or fibrous paper.

Flower symbolism is secondary unless requested. Visual role, real form, stem behavior, availability, and construction come first.

## 3. Buildability gate

For every flower, foliage, branch, and special material record:

- common market name; avoid invented cultivar precision;
- color and whether it is natural, dyed, sprayed, bleached, preserved, dried, or artificial;
- visual job: focal, mass, line, filler, highlight, or anchor;
- approximate stem count or quantity;
- ordinary/seasonal/special-order sourcing level;
- one substitute with the same form and visual job.

Default to fresh or commercially available dried/preserved material. Do not use an artificial flower unless the user permits it. If a source color cannot occur naturally, choose one of these in order:

1. move the large color field to wrapping paper;
2. use a naturally adjacent flower color;
3. use a real florist dye or spray on a limited material and disclose it;
4. redesign the palette relationship rather than inventing a flower.

Before generation confirm:

- every focal flower head has a believable supporting stem;
- stems travel in compatible directions and converge at one binding point;
- heavy heads are supported and do not float outside the bundle;
- the stem bundle is small enough for one adult hand to grip;
- the overall height and width are plausible for the stated stem counts;
- sharp, brittle, toxic, wet, or painted materials are handled in a plausible florist-safe way;
- no flower changes species, petal structure, or stem type merely to match the source.

## 4. Design the paper as scenery and structure

Specify visible layers from inside to outside:

1. hydration/protective layer when needed;
2. inner translucent or textured layer;
3. dominant backing sheet;
4. dark frame/outer sheet if present;
5. ribbon or tie.

For every layer state material, color, finish, opacity, purpose, and fold direction. Useful materials include matte florist paper, tissue, glassine, florist cellophane, vellum-like film, fibrous mulberry paper, kraft, mesh, organza, and perforated paper.

Paper must wrap around the real stem bundle. It may form large color planes, but it cannot float behind the bouquet as disconnected scenery. Show compression and small wrinkles near the hand and binding point.

## 5. Silhouette for a hand-carried bouquet

- Default to an imperfect asymmetric fan or a source-derived shape that still converges into one low binding point.
- Keep the bouquet's center of mass above or slightly in front of the hand, not far outside the grip.
- Allow enough exposed tied stem area for the left hand to hold without covering the entire construction.
- Preserve source negative space between stems while keeping the bundle mechanically connected.
- Large flowers sit closer to the structural center; long line flowers and light fillers may extend outward.

## 6. Final translation check

Hide the source and ask whether the bouquet still evokes its palette proportion, line skeleton, texture, negative space, focal placement, and mood. Then hide the flower heads and ask whether the remaining stems, paper, tie, and grip still describe a constructible bouquet. If either test fails, revise before generation.
